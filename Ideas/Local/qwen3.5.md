Here is a comprehensive analysis of your current website's content strategy and recommendations for rebuilding it into a modern, engaging platform for your Go club.

I have structured this into three parts: **What to Remove**, **Core Content Essentials**, and **Modern Feature Suggestions**.

---

### 1. What to Remove (The "Old Site" Audit)
Based on typical legacy websites from the early 2000s, here are elements that likely clutter your current page and should be streamlined or removed:

*   **Excessive Text Walls:** Old sites often have long paragraphs of text without breaks. Go is a visual game; users prefer scanning information quickly. Break content into bite-sized chunks with clear headings.
*   **Generic "Welcome" Pages:** A static "Welcome to the Lubelski Klub Go" header that doesn't say anything specific about *what happens there*. Replace this with an immediate hook (e.g., "Play, Learn, and Connect").
*   **Outdated Contact Methods:** If you have a physical address but no map link, or just a generic email without a form, update these. Modern users expect click-to-call or one-click email links.
*   **Static Image Galleries:** A gallery of random photos from 2015 is less useful than a curated "Recent Activity" section showing recent tournaments or social events.
*   **Overly Detailed Rulebooks:** If you have the full text of the *Rules of Go*, link to an external resource (like WGO) instead of hosting it all on your site. Keep only a quick summary for beginners.
*   **Broken or Dead Links:** Old sites often link to forums that are no longer active. Audit and remove links that lead nowhere.

---

### 2. Core Content Essentials (What to Keep & Enhance)
These are the non-negotiable elements every local Go club needs to function effectively:

*   **The "First Visit" Guide:** A dedicated section for new players explaining exactly what they need to do upon arrival (e.g., "Arrive 15 minutes early," "Bring a pen and paper," "Wear comfortable shoes").
*   **Location & Logistics:**
    *   Clear map integration (Google Maps/Apple Maps).
    *   Parking instructions.
    *   Public transport details.
    *   Accessibility info (e.g., elevator access, wheelchair-friendly seating).
*   **Schedule & Calendar:** A clear view of when the club meets (weekly/monthly) and any seasonal breaks.
*   **Upcoming Events:** Highlight tournaments, beginner workshops, or special guest games. Use a visual calendar if possible.
*   **Membership/Joining Info:** Clear instructions on how to become a member. Is it free? Do you need to buy a board set? What is the cost of entry for guests?
*   **Team/Staff Profiles:** Introduce key members (e.g., "The Sensei," "Tournament Organizer"). People connect with people, not just abstract organizations.

---

### 3. Modern Feature Suggestions (New Ideas)
To make your site feel current and engaging, consider adding these interactive or visual elements:

*   **Mobile-First Design:** Ensure the layout works perfectly on phones. Many players will check schedules while commuting to the club.
*   **"Quick Start" for Beginners:** A dedicated section with links to beginner tutorials (e.g., "How to play your first game," "Basic terminology"). This lowers the barrier to entry for new players.
*   **Digital Sign-Up Form:** Instead of just an email address, use a simple form (like Google Forms or Typeform) for event registration. It makes tracking attendance easier.
*   **Social Media Integration:** Embed links to your Discord server, Telegram group, or Instagram feed. This creates a sense of community beyond the physical meeting space.
*   **Photo/Video Highlights:** Instead of a static gallery, consider embedding short videos from recent games (with permission) or a "Game of the Month" feature.
*   **FAQ Section:** Anticipate common questions:
    *   *"Do I need to know Go already?"*
    *   *"What if I'm late?"*
    *   *"Can I bring my own board?"*

---

### 4. Suggested Information Architecture (Site Map)
Here is a proposed structure for your new pages:

1.  **Home:** Hero image, quick schedule, "How to Join" CTA, latest news.
2.  **About Us:** Mission statement, history of the club, team bios.
3.  **For Beginners:** Rules summary, equipment guide, beginner tutorials.
4.  **Events & Calendar:** Upcoming tournaments, workshops, and regular meetings.
5.  **Gallery/News:** Recent photos, tournament results, blog posts about local Go news.
6.  **Contact:** Map, email form, social links.
