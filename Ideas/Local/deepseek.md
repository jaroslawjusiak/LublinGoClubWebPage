Okay, running a local Go club is a fantastic goal! The website being built around 20 years ago tells me there's definitely room for a modern update. Let's focus purely on the content and user experience aspects first.

Reimagining your Go club webpage involves more than just updating graphics – it's about clearly presenting who you are, what happens there, and making it easy for interested people (both beginners and seasoned players) to find their way in or get involved. Here’s a breakdown of ideas:

Current Page (Implied):

Outdated Aesthetics: The design language is likely old-fashioned.
Poor Navigation: May be confusing, cluttered, or rely on guesswork rather than clear menu structure.
Unclear Value Proposition: Not obvious why someone should join or care about this specific club beyond "we play Go".
Lack of Engagement: No easy way to participate virtually (online forum, Discord?) or get involved locally if they can't attend often.
Insufficient Information/Welcome: Maybe it just throws you into the rules without a friendly introduction.
Here's what I'd suggest removing from the current site and what would be added in a new design:

Removal (Address these issues)
Outdated Visuals & Layout: Get rid of any graphics, colors, or layout that feels retro.
Confusing Navigation Menus: Simplify menus; avoid deep nested lists if possible. Make the most important sections (e.g., About Us, Events, Join) prominent and easy to find on every page.
Vague "Welcome" Statement: If there's a generic welcome banner, ditch it for something more specific or useful like an events list, upcoming game schedule, or relevant news.
Incomplete Information (for beginners): Don't just link to Go basics – summarize the key points here on the homepage in easy-to-understand language.
Lack of Clear "Join" Path: If it's not immediately obvious how to become a member or sign up, remove any assumptions and provide clear steps (link to membership form? Mention fees?).
Isolated Event Listings: Maybe events are listed but aren't linked directly into the main navigation flow.
New Content & Features for the Rebuilt Page
Modern, Clean Design:

Visual Appeal: Use contemporary web design principles with appealing visuals (maybe Go-themed images, diagrams of games, photos of members). Ensure good typography and readability.
Responsive Layout: Look great on all devices – computers, tablets, phones.
Crystal Clear Homepage - "The First Impression"

Hero Section: A large, eye-catching area introducing the club concisely (e.g., a compelling headline like "A Loving Home for Go Players!", sub-headline mentioning it's local or online-friendly).
What is Go? Snippet: Short, engaging description of what makes Go unique and fun. Mention basic concepts if needed without overwhelming.
Club Mission/Vision (Optional): What does your club strive to achieve?
Key Activities Overview: List the main things you do – e.g., "Weekly Games", "Beginner Workshops", "Tournaments".
Prominent Call-to-Actions (CTAs):
Become a Member - Clear link.
Find Game Times - Link directly to schedule/event listing.
Learn More About Go - Link to introductory resources.
Structured Information Architecture:

About Us Section: Detail the club's history (if relevant), its goals, who runs it (mention key organizers or regulars briefly).
Who We Are/Meet Our Players/Membership Info: Explain the different levels of players you cater to (beginners, intermediates, experts) and perhaps highlight some active members. Maybe include a section on "Joining Us".
Upcoming Events / Game Schedule:
This should be highly visible, perhaps even in its own tab or as part of the homepage.
List current and upcoming events clearly (time, date, location/online link).
Include specific game times/day passes if you have frequent casual games. Maybe a simple calendar view.
Resources Section:
Beginner Resources: Links to simplified Go rules explanations (e.g., from official sites or beginner-friendly blogs), lists of learning materials, maybe links to online courses or local beginners groups.
Advanced Resources / Library: Link to game databases you use (like KGS/Osuwa/KataGo/GnuGo boards? - clarify which ones). Provide pointers for good opening books or strategy guides relevant to your club's level. Offer help contacts if needed.
News & Announcements: A dedicated section for important club news, upcoming events reminders, results of recent games/tournaments (if applicable).
Community Engagement Features:

Discussion Forum/Discussion Area: An essential part! Allow members and interested players to chat about Go, ask questions, discuss strategy.
Contact Page: Easy way for people who aren't ready to join via email or message form specifically related to the club's needs (e.g., "Venue Feedback", "Membership Inquiries").
Simple Online Presence:
If you have a Discord server or another online community, provide clear links.
Maybe offer a way for people who can't attend physically that day to spectate via screen share link if applicable.
