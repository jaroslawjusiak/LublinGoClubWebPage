Since I cannot "browse" the live site in the same way a human does (interact with buttons, see animations, etc.), I will base my assessment on the common characteristics of older community-led hobby sites and the standard UX/UI principles for modern "Club" websites.

To modernize the site, we need to move from a **"Digital Bulletin Board"** (where information is just dumped) to a **"Community Portal"** (where the user feels welcomed and guided).

Here is my proposal for the content overhaul:

---

### 1. What to Remove (The "Cleanup")
*   **The "Wall of Text":** Old sites often try to explain everything on the homepage. We should remove dense paragraphs. People don't read on the web; they scan.
*   **Outdated Archives:** If there are news items or results from 2022 or earlier, move them to an "Archive" section or remove them. They clutter the current "vibe."
*   **Cluttered Sidebars:** Navigation should be clean. Remove any "widgets" or sidebars that distract from the main call to action.
*   **Low-Resolution/Stretched Images:** Any photos that look blurry or are distorted should be removed. We need high-quality, intentional visuals.
*   **Complex Navigation:** If a user has to click more than 3 times to find "How to join" or "When is the next meeting," the navigation is too complex.

---

### 2. New Content & Features (The "Upgrade")

I suggest focusing on three pillars: **Onboarding, Community, and Logistics.**

#### A. The "First-Time Visitor" Experience (Onboarding)
*   **The "Are you a Beginner?" Section:** Go can be intimidating. A dedicated section for "Newbies" explaining that no experience is required is crucial for growth.
*   **"How to Play" Mini-Guide:** A very brief, visual summary of the goal of the game. 
*   **The "Path to Join":** A simple 3-step visual (e.g., 1. Visit us / 2. Play a game / 3. Join the club).

#### B. Community & Personality (The "Vibe")
*   **Member Spotlights:** Instead of just a list of names, feature a "Player of the Month" or a "New Member Welcome" with a small photo and a quote about their favorite opening or why they love Go.
*   **Gallery of Real Moments:** Move away from stock photos. Show photos of your actual club members playing, laughing, or drinking coffee. This builds trust.
*   **Testimonials:** A simple quote from a long-time member about why they enjoy the club's atmosphere.

#### C. Logistics & Engagement (The "Utility")
*   **Live/Upcoming Schedule:** A clear, prominent "Next Meeting" card with date, time, and location (integrated with a Google Maps link).
*   **Difficulty Levels:** Clearly state that the club accommodates different levels (e.g., "From 10kyu to 1-dan"). This prevents experts from being bored and beginners from being scared.
*   **Join/Contact CTA (Call to Action):** A prominent button in the header (e.g., "Join our next session" or "Message the Organizer").
*   **Social/Messaging Integration:** If you use Discord, WhatsApp, or Telegram, have a clear button to join the "Live Chat."

---

### 3. Suggested Information Architecture (The Sitemap)
Instead of a messy homepage, I suggest a clean 4-page structure:

1.  **Home:** The "Hook." High-quality image, "Next Meeting" info, "Who we are," and a "Join Us" button.
2.  **About/Newcomers:** The "Education." Rules, beginner info, and history of the club.
3.  **Gallery/News:** The "Proof." Photos of events and recent results.
4.  **Contact/Location:** The "Action." Map, contact form, and social media links.

---

### 4. Visual Strategy (The "Look & Feel")
*   **Minimalism:** Use lots of "white space" (negative space). It makes the site feel modern and premium.
*   **Color Palette:** Stick to 3 main colors (e.g., a deep "Go board" wood brown, a stone white/grey, and one accent color like a forest green or a deep red).
*   **Typography:** Switch to a clean, sans-serif font for readability, using bold headers to separate sections.
