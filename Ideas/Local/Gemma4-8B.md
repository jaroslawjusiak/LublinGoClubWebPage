## 🗑️ Content Strategy: What to Remove or De-emphasize

The goal when removing content isn't just deleting; it's *pruning* anything that distracts from the primary calls-to-action (CTA). If a piece of information requires more than two clicks to find, consider if it belongs on the homepage.

*   **❌ Dense Historical Text Blocks:** Overly long "Our History" sections are often beautiful but bad for web readability. Instead of narrative paragraphs detailing every founder or historical event, summarize this into 2-3 powerful mission statement bullet points: *Why do we exist? What do we value?*
    *(Action: Turn history into a brief, punchy timeline graphic on the "About" page.)*
*   **❌ Generic Boilerplate Imagery/Graphics:** If the current site uses outdated or overly formal graphics (e.g., clip art, very institutional logos), these distract from the fun and passion of the hobby. Replace them with authentic photos of people playing Go.
    *(Action: Photos are always more powerful than stock images.)*
*   **❌ Overly Complex Navigation:** If there are too many links spread across deep menus (e.g., "Advanced Topics," "Tournament Archives 2015-2018"), consolidate them. Group related items under clear, simple headers (e.g., "Programs" instead of having five different submenus).
*   **❌ Passive Language/Tone:** Avoid sounding academic or overly formal. The tone should be enthusiastic, welcoming, and slightly casual—like a conversation between club members.

***

## ✨ Content Strategy: New Features & Core Pillars

The new site needs to be organized around the user journey. I suggest structuring the content into five main pillars that serve different audiences (Newbies, Regulars, Parents/Families).

### 🎯 Pillar 1: The Hero Section (Immediate Focus)
This is the first thing a visitor sees. It must answer "When and Where?" instantly.

*   **Live Status/Next Event Spotlight:** Instead of just listing dates, feature the *next* meeting prominently (e.g., "Today! Casual Play at [Location]. Doors open 18:00.").
*   **Simple CTA Buttons:** These should be impossible to miss: **[Join Us!]**, **[View Calendar]**, **[Learn About Go]**.

### 🎓 Pillar 2: The Beginner Experience (The Conversion Funnel)
This content is designed to bring in the new, intimidated player. It needs to remove fear and confusion.

*   **"What is Go? (For Non-Players)":** A short, visually rich section that explains the game simply—without being condescending. Use analogy (e.g., "It's like chess, but with area control").
*   **The Starter Ladder/Program:** Create a simple path: *Curious → Trial Player → Member.* Offer resources for the trial player: e.g., "Free Introductory Lesson This Month" or "Recommended Beginner Kit."
*   **FAQ Section (Highly Recommended):** Address logistical questions immediately: "Do I need equipment?", "How much does it cost?", "What should I bring?"

### 🗓️ Pillar 3: Community & Activity (The Proof of Life)
This shows that the club is active, fun, and welcoming. People join clubs because they like the people, not just the hobby.

*   **Visual Gallery:** A rotating carousel or grid of high-quality photos from recent meetings. Show smiling faces, action shots of players, and successful tournaments. *Crucial for establishing a friendly atmosphere.*
*   **Tournament Results Feed:** Don't just list results; celebrate them. Highlight the winners, post their photos (with permission), and include brief congratulatory messages. This creates anticipation for the next event.
*   **The Coach/Mentor Spotlight:** If you have experienced players or dedicated mentors, dedicate a small section to them. Put up their picture and a short bio. This humanizes the club.

### ⚙️ Pillar 4: Practical Utility (Membership & Logistics)
Make joining friction-free.

*   **Simple Membership Flowchart/Application:** Instead of an intimidating form, make it seem like a guided process: *Step 1: Attend a Trial → Step 2: Speak to Mentor X → Step 3: Become a Member.* Provide the contact person for each step.
*   **Equipment Rental/Buying Info:** If the club handles or recommends equipment (stones, boards), this information needs to be centralized here.

### 💡 Pillar 5: Thought Leadership & Engagement (Ongoing Content)
To keep people coming back, you need fresh content that provides value outside of just "Meeting Times."

*   **Club Blog / News Feed:** Use this for mini-articles:
    *   "Three Things to Remember When You Start Playing Go."
    *   "Reviewing the Tournament Format: How Did It Work?"
    *   Interviews with high-level players or local experts.
*   **The Calendar/Schedule (Master View):** Must be robust, allowing users to filter by event type (Casual Play / Competition / Beginner Workshop).
