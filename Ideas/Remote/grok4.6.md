### What to Remove or Heavily Revise from the Current Site

1. **The ultra-minimal “just the facts” flyer style**  
   The entire homepage is basically one short block of text. Remove the bare-bones presentation. Keep the core facts (time, place, free) but embed them in richer context so they don’t feel like the whole story.

2. **The incomplete / text-only rules page as the main learning resource**  
   The current rules explanation is a wall of text with figure references that don’t fully deliver. It’s better suited as a starting point or appendix, not the primary “learn Go” offering. Either scrap it as a standalone page or completely overhaul it into something visual, interactive, and progressive (or replace it with curated external + club-specific materials).

3. **Lack of any personality, history, or social proof**  
   There’s no “who we are,” no photos of people playing, no mention of past events (you do organize tournaments — your Facebook shows this), and almost no contact beyond the address. This makes the club feel invisible or inactive.

4. **No clear call-to-action beyond “come on Wednesday” and “read the rules”**  
   The page doesn’t guide different visitor types (total beginner, parent with kids, experienced player visiting Lublin, someone wanting to help organize).

5. **Outdated or sparse language**  
   Phrases like “SPOTKANIA SĄ DARMOWE !!!” work for urgency but feel shouty and dated. Soften and modernize while keeping the friendly, inclusive tone.

**Keep and strengthen**: The core message of free, family-friendly meetings open to all ages. The welcoming attitude toward parents + kids learning together is a real strength — lean into it hard.

### Suggested New Content & Features

#### 1. Strong Homepage Hero + Clear Value Proposition
- Short, warm intro: What is Lubelski Klub GO? (A friendly community in Lublin that meets weekly to play and learn the ancient game of Go — free, open to absolute beginners, kids, families, and stronger players.)
- Highlight unique selling points: Free, multi-generational, patient teaching, family atmosphere, location in a cultural center.
- Prominent “Next meeting” box with date/time/place + “Come for the first time — no equipment or knowledge needed.”
- Quick links: “I’m a complete beginner” / “I have kids” / “I already play” / “Contact us.”

#### 2. About the Club / Our Story
- Short history (when founded, how it started, growth).
- Who runs it / key people (with photos and short bios if they’re comfortable — “Meet the organizers”).
- Philosophy: Learning together, fun first, improvement second, inclusive atmosphere.
- Photos or short descriptions of typical evenings (casual games, teaching kids, family boards, stronger players analyzing).
- Membership stats or vibe: “We range from 5-year-olds to retirees; absolute beginners to dan-level players.”

#### 3. Meetings & Practical Info (Expanded)
- Detailed location section: Address, how to find Sala 14 / Galeria na górze, parking/public transport tips, photos of the entrance/room, Google Maps embed description.
- What to expect on your first visit (step-by-step: arrive, someone will greet you, we have boards/stones, you can watch or play immediately on 9x9, etc.).
- Equipment: We provide everything; bring nothing.
- Schedule exceptions, holidays, summer changes.
- Optional: Simple calendar view of upcoming regular meetings + special events.

#### 4. Learning Resources (Much Stronger Than Current Rules)
- Progressive “Learn Go” section:
  - Absolute beginner path (5–10 minute animated or illustrated basics + “first game” guide).
  - Visual rules with clear diagrams (capture, liberty, territory, ko, eyes, etc.).
  - Club-specific teaching style notes (“We start everyone on 9x9…”).
  - Recommended external free resources (Sensei’s Library basics, online servers like OGS, YouTube channels in Polish/English, apps).
  - “Problems of the week” or simple tsumego suitable for club level.
  - Downloadable one-page “Go cheatsheet” or printable beginner board diagrams.
- For kids/parents: Special “Go for children” page — benefits (concentration, logic, patience, cultural curiosity), age recommendations, how parents can play with kids at home.

#### 5. Events, Tournaments & News / Blog
- Upcoming events calendar (club tournaments, open days, workshops, joint events with other clubs).
- Past events archive with results, photos, short recaps (you already do tournaments — celebrate them!).
- Simple news/blog: “Tournament recap,” “New teaching materials,” “Member spotlight,” “Go in Lublin history,” “Recommended books/movies.”
- This turns the site into a living hub instead of a static notice.

#### 6. Community & Social Proof
- Photo gallery (meetings, tournaments, smiling faces, kids playing, boards in action). Respect privacy — get consent or use group shots.
- Testimonials / short quotes: “My 8-year-old loves it…”, “I came as a complete beginner last year…”, “Great family activity.”
- Member ranks or ladder (optional, light version) or “Players of all levels welcome.”
- Links to Facebook, any Discord/Telegram, phone number, email.
- “Bring a friend” or “How to get involved” (help with teaching, organize side events, contribute content).

#### 7. For Visitors & Special Audiences
- “Visiting Lublin?” section for travelers/players from other cities.
- Partnerships or links: Other Polish Go clubs, Polish Go Association, European events.
- Accessibility notes (if relevant).
- FAQ: Do I need to know the rules? Is it really free? Can I bring my own board? What ages? How strong are the players? Is there ranking? etc.

#### 8. Additional Engaging / Useful Features
- Interactive or embedded Go board demo (even a simple static “try placing a stone” explanation or link to an online beginner tool).
- “Why Go?” short section — history (brief), benefits (mindfulness, strategy, cross-cultural), fun facts, famous players or Go in culture (Hikaru no Go mention if it fits the audience).
- Resources for stronger players: Joseki links, game records from club games (SGF if available), recommended study paths.
- Contact / Join form (simple “I’m interested / first visit notification” so you can prepare a welcome).
- Multilingual note or basic English version of key pages (useful for expats/students in Lublin and international Go players).
- “Support the club” subtle section (if you ever need equipment donations, voluntary contributions, or 1.5% tax designation — only if relevant and tasteful).

#### 9. Tone & Storytelling Guidelines
- Warm, inclusive, patient, slightly playful (Go has deep culture but the club feels joyful and family-oriented).
- Polish-first, clear language, short paragraphs.
- Emphasize “no pressure, come as you are.”
- Show rather than just tell: photos + short stories beat long claims.
