I took a look at the current page, and I have to agree—it's a bit of a digital fossil. The good news is that this is a fantastic opportunity. The current page gives us a clear starting point, and there's a lot of great inspiration to draw from other clubs and modern web practices.

Here's a breakdown of my initial ideas for the new site's content, structured into what to remove, what to add, and some new features to make it the heart of your club's community.

### 🗑️ What to Remove: Decluttering for Clarity

The current page is essentially a single block of text. The main issue is that it tries to be everything at once. We should remove this monolithic structure and break the information down into clear, distinct sections. The goal is to make it instantly scannable so a visitor can find what they need in seconds.

*   **The Text Block:** Replace the single paragraph with a proper homepage layout. The essential details (who, what, when, where) should be immediately visible in a "hero" section or "at-a-glance" cards, with links to deeper pages for more info.
*   **The "Zasady Gry" Link:** Right now, the link to the rules is buried in the text. This is a crucial piece of information for newcomers and deserves its own prominent place in the navigation menu, not just a link at the end of a paragraph.

### 🚀 What to Add: Building a Welcoming Hub

The current site tells people the club exists. The new site should show them **why they should join** and make the first step as easy as possible. We can do this by building out dedicated pages.

#### For Newcomers & Beginners (Your Highest Priority Audience)
*   **A Dedicated "First Visit" or "For Beginners" Page:** This is the most important page to get right. It should answer all the questions a new person might have.
    *   **What to Expect:** A simple, reassuring guide to a typical club evening. What should they bring? (Just themselves!) What will happen? (They'll be paired with a friendly member who can teach the basics). How long do games last? (On a small board, a beginner game might only take 15 minutes).
    *   **The Core Rules:** A clear, visual, and simple guide to the rules of Go. Using a 9x9 or 13x13 board for explanation is much less intimidating than a full 19x19 board. You could embed an interactive tutorial.
    *   **Your Club's Approach:** Explicitly state your club's philosophy. The current text says "We learn and play together, often as entire families," which is wonderful! This should be a headline, not a footnote. Reassure them that all levels are welcome, from complete beginners to experienced players.
*   **A Clear "Join Us" / "Contact" Page:** The current page gives a location and time, but that's it. Create a simple contact form so people can ask questions before they come. This removes a huge barrier for shy newcomers. You could also add a link to your Discord or WhatsApp group if you have one.

#### For Regular Members & the Local Community
*   **Club News & Blog:** A simple blog or "News" section is a fantastic way to keep members engaged and show the public what you do. You can post recaps of tournaments, share photos from club nights, announce upcoming events, or even write short strategy pieces.
*   **Tournaments & Events Calendar:** A dedicated page listing all your club's activities. This could include weekly meetings, special workshops, and any tournaments you organize. You can even post past tournament results and photos, which creates a valuable historical record for the club.
*   **A "Members" or "Community" Section:** A simple page introducing the club's organizers or key members with a short bio can make the club feel much more personal and welcoming. You could also include a "Club Ladder" or a friendly internal ranking to add a bit of fun and competition.

#### For the Curious Public
*   **An Expanded "About Us" Page:** Go beyond just the meeting details. Share your club's history. When was it founded? By whom? A personal story is much more engaging than a dry mission statement. You can also include a section explaining what Go is to a complete outsider—its history, its cultural significance, and why it's so addictive!

### ✨ New Features: Ideas to Make the Site Shine

Beyond just the core pages, here are some features that could really elevate the new site and make it a more dynamic and useful tool for your club.

*   **A "Learn Go" Interactive Section:** Embed a simple, interactive Go board using a library like **WGo.js** or **Besogo**. You could set up beginner problems (tsumego) and simple guided lessons directly on the site. This is a huge value-add for beginners and sets your club apart.
*   **Game Record Library:** Allow members to submit their game records (SGF files) to be shared and reviewed. You could create a "Game of the Week" section where a stronger player provides a short commentary on a member's game. This is a fantastic learning tool for everyone.
*   **A Club Ladder or Internal League:** Use a simple web app or even a spreadsheet to manage a club ladder. This encourages members to play a variety of opponents and adds a fun, competitive element to regular club nights. It also gives people a reason to check the website regularly to see their ranking.
*   **Integration with the Local Community:** If the club meets in a public venue like the **Młodzieżowy Dom Kultury**, you could create a small, dedicated landing page for their website or newsletter, introducing the club as a part of their offerings.

### 💎 Summary of Key Ideas

To put it simply, the goal is to transform the site from a **static noticeboard** into a **dynamic, welcoming front door** for your club. The priority is to make it incredibly easy for a nervous beginner to find all the information they need and feel excited to show up.

| Remove | Add |
| :--- | :--- |

| The buried rules link | A prominent "For Beginners" page with a simple rules guide |
| The lack of a contact method | A dedicated "Contact/Join Us" page with a form |
